# Visual Master Review

Use this checklist after generating candidate visual master images.

## Choose for Long-Term Use, Not One-Shot Appeal

Select the direction that is easiest to reuse across many articles, not necessarily the most decorative image.

## Review Criteria

### Identity

- The character is immediately recognizable.
- Body shape and face can be described in stable words.
- Outfit and palette are distinctive but not overly complex.
- For black-and-white rendering, the character remains recognizable through silhouette, clothing/prop shape, and stable light-midtone-dark anchors rather than hue alone.
- The character can perform many actions without losing identity.

### Production Stability

- The style is easy for future prompts to reproduce.
- The character is not dependent on a specific background or pose.
- The image does not contain too much text that future generations might copy.
- Props support the domain but do not become mandatory clutter.

### Scenario Fit

- The IP can participate in problem breakdowns.
- The IP can diagnose, compare, organize, explain, and navigate.
- The IP can appear in both covers and inline article illustrations.
- The character reads well at small web-article sizes.

### Risks

Mark any generated detail as "do not lock" if it should not become part of the stable IP, such as:

- exact whiteboard wording
- accidental logo text
- overly specific badge copy
- decorative props
- background layout
- one-off facial expression

## Review Output

After review, write:

- Accept / revise / reject.
- What identity traits are locked.
- What details are useful but optional.
- What details should be avoided in future images.
- Next image needed, such as action pose, expression sheet, or article scene test.
